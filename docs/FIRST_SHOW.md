# EmberBanter: first show, ready to rehearse

Prepared November 16, 2026, America/New_York. Draft; no broadcast date set.

**Clock out. Queue up.** After-work gaming, horror nights and honest conversation.

This is a performance guide with flexible gameplay, not 90 minutes of forced reading. Choose a game you already own. Replace bracketed fields before recording. No personal anecdote or viewer response below is claimed to have happened.

## The 90-minute stream

| Elapsed time | Action | Host cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 | Starting screen; check meters privately | Countdown has no audio. READY does not switch scenes or go live. |
| 3–7 | Welcome and one clear challenge | Read the opening below, then explain the actual game objective. |
| 7–25 | First attempt | Say what you notice, what you choose, and why. Let important game dialogue breathe. |
| 25–30 | Chat or solo conversation | Ask one concrete question; use the no-chat version if needed. |
| 30–50 | Second attempt | Change one decision from the first attempt and explain it. |
| 50–53 | BRB | Switch deliberately; verify intended mic/game/guest audio is muted. |
| 53–75 | Return and final challenge | Briefly reset the premise for anyone arriving late. |
| 75–85 | Best moment and final round | Describe a real turning point. Mark the actual recording time after it happens. |
| 85–90 | Close | One invitation, a confirmed next session only if known, deliberate stop. |

### Opening

“Welcome to EmberBanter. I'm Dam. Clock out. Queue up.

Tonight we're playing [GAME], and the plan is [ONE SPECIFIC CHALLENGE]. Whether that plan survives contact with my decision-making is another question.

This is the after-work corner: good games, honest conversation, and the occasional choice we probably should have discussed first.

Quick question before we get into it: when you get home, do you want a game that relaxes you—or one that gives you a completely different problem? Mine tonight is [ACTUAL ANSWER]. Let's go.”

### Commentary loop

Use these as cues, not lines to repeat every minute:

- **Notice:** “That sound came from [ACTUAL DIRECTION].”
- **Choose:** “I'm going [ACTUAL ROUTE] because [REASON].”
- **Predict:** “If that door opens, I'm expecting [YOUR REAL GUESS].”
- **React:** Give your natural response, then explain enough for a new viewer to follow.
- **Reset:** “The goal is still [GOAL]. That attempt taught me [OBSERVATION].”

### Conversation break

“What makes a horror game work for you: not knowing what's coming, or knowing exactly what's coming and having to walk toward it anyway?”

If chat is quiet: “I'll give you mine. [YOUR ACTUAL OPINION]. I'm curious whether that changes once we learn this game's rules.”

If someone answers: respond to the real answer, ask one follow-up, then return to play. Do not invent chat messages or pressure silent viewers to speak.

### Recovery lines

- Technical issue: “The picture's taking a break without filing the paperwork. Give me a minute to fix it.” Switch to BRB before troubleshooting anything private.
- After a mistake: “That was research. Very expensive research.” Explain the actual mistake once, then continue.
- Uncomfortable chat: “We can disagree. Keep it about the game and leave personal attacks out.” Enforce channel rules without prolonging an argument.
- Return from BRB: “Back. We're trying [GOAL], and the adjustment is [REAL CHANGE].”

### Close

“That was EmberBanter. Tonight's best moment was [REAL MOMENT], and the lesson is [YOUR REAL TAKEAWAY].

If this is your kind of after-work hangout, follow the channel. [ONLY IF CONFIRMED: Next time is DATE, TIME EASTERN.] Thanks for spending part of your night here. Clock out. Queue up.”

Switch to Ending, check that the desired audio state is correct, then stop the broadcast manually. The scene does not stop it.

## Three clip concepts, currently untimed

| Concept | Opening idea | Keep the context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The confident wrong turn | Begin immediately before your actual prediction fails. | Include enough setup to understand the mistake and your real reaction. |
| The after-work question | Put a clear, specific opinion in the first sentence. | Keep the reason; avoid turning a joke into an apparent claim about someone. |
| The earned recovery | Show the problem, your changed decision and the payoff. | Do not manufacture a win from separate attempts. |

These are suggestions, not generated clips or quotes. Record actual in/out seconds after reviewing the file. Aim initially for 20–45 seconds when the moment supports it; that is an editorial test, not an algorithm rule. Use one accurate title and corrected captions. Keep the HUD and subtitles visible.

## Separate 35-minute podcast pilot

Working title: **When does a horror game stop being scary?**

Alternate titles: **The moment fear becomes homework**; **Why the quiet corridor wins**.

| Time | Beat | Prompts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Hook | “A monster can be terrifying once. What keeps it scary the tenth time?” |
| 2–7 | Your own example | Describe one game you actually played; explain the scene in words for audio listeners. |
| 7–17 | Uncertainty versus repetition | What did you know? What could you control? When did you learn the pattern? |
| 17–27 | Comfort and stress after work | Do you want challenge, company or relief? Give an honest example, not a claim about every player. |
| 27–32 | Counterargument | Can a predictable enemy still work? How do sound, resource limits or a new goal change it? |
| 32–35 | Takeaway and close | Name your one strongest conclusion and ask listeners for a specific counterexample. |

These are original opinion prompts. Verify any added claims about release dates, sales, psychology or developer intent before recording. Remove references that require seeing the screen. No episode, guest invitation or feed submission has been made.
