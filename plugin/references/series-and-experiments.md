# Show structures and first experiment

Proposals dated November 16, 2026. No sessions scheduled or published.

## Series menu

| Series | Viewer promise | Repeatable mechanic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clocked Out, Queued Up | One game challenge after a long day | State one objective; offer route A/B; recap the attempt honestly. |
| Last Call for Horror | A tense run with a host who talks through the bad decisions | One scary objective, one rule, one visible progress marker. |
| The Cooldown | A complete conversation after the game | One question, two viewpoints, one personal takeaway; verify factual claims. |

Start with the first series, with horror as a game choice. The other titles are segment concepts, not cleared separate brands.

## 90-minute live run sheet

| Minutes | Action | Ready-to-use host line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Hook, challenge and rule | “I clocked out of one stressful situation and loaded another. Tonight I've got one objective: [real game objective]. You get to choose the route.” |
| 2–8 | Minimal setup and first choice | “Safe route or terrible idea? If chat's quiet, I'm taking the terrible idea and accepting responsibility.” |
| 8–35 | Attempt one; narrate decisions | “Here's what I think happens if I go left. Let's find out how wrong I am.” |
| 35–40 | Natural pause, recap, water | “The plan was solid. The execution has filed a complaint. Here's what actually changed.” |
| 40–70 | Escalate or revise one rule | “Same goal, one change: [real constraint]. Last attempt taught us [actual observation].” |
| 70–85 | Final attempt and payoff | “Win or lose, we're closing this loop. Here's the decision that matters.” |
| 85–90 | Honest recap, one next-show idea | “Best moment: [real event]. Worst decision: [real event]. Next time, would you rather see [A] or [B]?” |

Silence fallback: explain what you see, predict a consequence, act, compare prediction with outcome. Never invent viewers, messages, donations or live polls. Take breaks as needed; the timings are flexible.

Clip recipe, **untimed concept**: “I had one job” opening → enough context to understand the task → actual failure/success → one reaction line. Alternative: a 65–90-second complete story about the game's most surprising decision. No timestamps exist until the recording does.

## Standalone 35-minute conversation

0–2: a clear question and why Dam cares. 2–8: first personal story. 8–18: strongest opposing viewpoint and specific examples. 18–28: what changed the host's mind or what remains unresolved. 28–33: practical takeaway. 33–35: brief close. Research any game release, film, health or money fact separately; personal experience is labeled as personal experience.

## Eight-session opening test

Hypothesis: showing the challenge immediately holds more opening viewers than beginning with a short story. A = challenge first, B = a relevant story of at most 20 seconds, then the same challenge. Predeclare A, B, B, A, A, B, B, A. This is a balanced exploratory sequence, not random assignment or a causal proof.

Hold game/series, general length, title style and feasible weekday/time window as steady as practical. Keep Shorts and live measures separate. At seven days compare platform-reported opening retention for replays where available, alongside live concurrent viewing and production effort. Record audience size and any outages or major game changes. If no eligible analytics exist, report insufficient evidence; do not fabricate a retention rate from public views.

Use the more sustainable opening provisionally if the direction repeats across comparable sessions. Small samples, returning viewers and changing traffic can confound the result. More observations or a native randomized platform experiment would be needed for stronger claims. No calendar entries or automations are created by this plan.
