# Repeatable shows, useful measurements

Developed November 16, 2026, America/New_York. Proposed editorial strategy; not observed channel performance or a disclosed platform ranking algorithm. Current primary sources are in [money and sources](monetization-and-sources.md).

## The audience promise

EmberBanter: after-work gaming and honest hangouts with Dam. Chef by day, questionable survival instincts by night. Start with one understandable series, not an unrelated topic every session. Horror, gaming and conversation fit when the same host promise connects them. Test the hypothesis rather than assuming a large market exists.

**Promise → stakes → choice → escalation → payoff → reason to return.** Every title promises an identifiable experience. Open with that experience, state what winning or losing means, offer one understandable choice, narrate your decisions and close the loop. A clip must deliver its own payoff; withholding every answer for a link is a weak viewer experience.

YouTube describes recommendation signals through appeal, engagement and satisfaction. It recommends clear packaging and an opening that fulfills the title's promise. Use this as a diagnostic, not a formula for guaranteed reach. Low starts suggest reviewing topic/title; early exits suggest reviewing opening/context; absent return viewing suggests reviewing the series promise. These are hypotheses, not proven causes. Tags mainly help with spelling variants, so prioritize the show itself.

## Proposed production loop

One 90-minute live session per week when the work rota permits; two good clips from it; one optional edited conversation only if it works by itself. Reserve roughly 30 minutes to prepare, 90 live, 60 to edit/review and 15 to inspect results: 3 hours 15 minutes as a planning allowance, not a measured production time. If editing exceeds the allowance, publish fewer worthwhile clips. Never manufacture a quote to meet a quota.

Proposed clip lengths: 20–45 seconds for one incident; 65–90 seconds for a story that needs context. These are editorial experiments, not monetization guarantees or universal optimum lengths. Check the destination's current rules before export. A podcast draft is about 35 minutes, only when there is a complete conversation rather than disconnected gameplay audio.

## Transparent editorial clip score

Rate each factor 0–5. The subjective score is `5×clarity + 4×context + 5×payoff + 3×audio + 3×legibility`, maximum 100. Weights are editorial judgment: clarity 25%, context 20%, payoff 25%, audio 15%, legibility 15%. A rating of 0 means absent/unusable, 3 workable with edits, 5 immediately understandable/usable. Score moments relative to the same session; never describe it as probability of virality, a trained model or a platform's algorithm.

A high score cannot rescue misleading context, wrong subtitles, unlicensed footage/music or inaudible dialogue. Record the source file and real in/out times. With no recording, label ideas **untimed concepts**. The bundled clip tools validate real cuts and require operator review before rendering.

## Metrics that answer a decision

| Question | Measure and denominator | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did people choose it? | Platform-reported impressions CTR within the same surface; separately, Shorts viewed vs swiped away where available | Those are different denominators; don't pool them. |
| Did they stay? | First-30-second retention for comparable videos; average duration and percentage viewed | Shorter clips can raise completion without increasing value. Compare similar lengths. |
| Did the live show work? | Average concurrent viewers and unique chatters within the session | Concurrent viewers aren't total unique viewers; quiet viewers still count. |
| Did they return? | Platform-reported returning viewers over the same reporting window | No cross-platform identity deduplication. Likes are only an imperfect proxy. |
| Was the effort sustainable? | Completed edits and actual production minutes per session | Don't count draft files as published outputs. |

Log at 24 hours and seven days from publication; compare at the same age. Keep missing values blank/null. Public like/view counts do not reveal another creator's retention or profit. Do not claim that copying a top creator's length, face or frequency reproduces their results.

Review a poor result against the actual recording: promise mismatch, long silence, game audio masking speech, confusing captions or a payoff that arrives too late. Change one major variable next. Keep the best part of the show instead of rebuilding the whole identity after one weak upload.
